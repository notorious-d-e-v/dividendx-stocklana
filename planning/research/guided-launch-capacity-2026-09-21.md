# Guided launch capacity audit — 21 September 2026

**Follow-up:** the user approved 500 starts/day targeting approximately $50/day. The resulting [metering contract](../../spec/guided-launch-metering.md) uses 16 simultaneous reservations and per-session counters, reducing normal GET traffic to one full-ledger read and no global counter write. The [release review](../guided-lazy-entry-review-2026-09-21.md) records validation and publication. The audit below preserves the original four-active/100-daily findings; its proposed three-read architecture and effort estimate are superseded by that contract.

Read-only source and pricing audit. No production sessions, quota edits, billing changes, or load tests were performed. This is a planning estimate, not a measured concurrency guarantee.

## Current behavior

`DEFAULT_LIMITS` in `packages/hosted-broker/src/contract.ts` sets **four active Sandbox reservations shared by wallet and guided**, 100 starts per UTC day globally, six per visitor cookie, 30 per observed IP, and a 30-second creation cooldown. Each reservation starts a 15-minute lifetime immediately, has a 90-second provisioning deadline, and retains a 24-hour tombstone. It allows 2,000 forwarded requests in total, 240 per minute, and 120 mutations. `SessionLedgerRepository.reserve()` in `packages/hosted-broker/src/ledger.ts` enforces the shared cap and all daily quotas; failed/uncertain creations have already consumed a daily start, and unknown potentially live providers may continue holding capacity until their recorded expiry. `holdsCapacity()` counts starting and resetting records. `HostedSessionBroker.start()` in `packages/hosted-broker/src/broker.ts` may poll readiness for eight seconds after the provider create/launch, and `VercelSandboxProvider` gives each provider operation a 15-second timeout. A visitor must explicitly start a session; a GET on the landing page issues a visitor cookie and reads the ledger, but does not consume a VM or daily start. Both domains issue distinct host-only cookies.

Every forwarded request, including guided `/state` GET and wallet reads, calls `ledger.charge()` before contacting the VM. This transaction reads, clones, and conditionally rewrites **one private Vercel Blob JSON object** for the entire fleet. It retries CAS conflicts at most 12 times with 5–24 ms jitter, then returns 503 `busy`. A previous eight-writer large-Blob probe found intermittent conditional-write conflicts; the exact Blob error was subsequently classified for retry ([hosting release review](../hosting-release-review.md)). The guided UI in `apps/web/src/demos/GuidedDemosApp.tsx` polls `/state` every 900 ms while pending/preparing/running, so one continuously active tour may create about 67 ledger writes per minute or about 1,000 over 15 minutes, below its 2,000 total allowance but substantial on the shared CAS object. Two browsers/tabs for one session double that rate; four continuously polling tabs can exceed its 240/minute budget. The initial session gate polls every two seconds for up to 90 seconds. There is no queue or reservation for visitors who meet the global cap: 503 is returned with Retry-After. Raising `active` alone would amplify global CAS contention and would not change the daily 100-start ceiling.

The provider creates a **nonpersistent**, `deny-all` VM from the accepted snapshot with two vCPUs and thus four GB provisioned memory (`packages/hosted-broker/src/provider.ts`). It uses the remaining reservation lifetime as the provider timeout; the separate server Function has a 60-second max duration (`vercel.json`). Do not turn off isolation or auto-repeat an uncertain start/reset to increase capacity. The public devnet faucet has independent, finite limits (30 global grants/day, 1 SOL lifetime endowment), so a social launch of the wallet flow can hit a separate gate even if guided capacity grows ([hosting operations](../../docs/hosting-operations.md)).

## Provider limits and indicative cost

The [Vercel Sandbox pricing and quotas page](https://vercel.com/docs/sandbox/pricing), last updated 10 September 2026, lists Pro concurrency of 10,000 Sandboxes and a dynamic creation allocation starting at 150 vCPUs/minute, ramping toward 5,000 vCPUs/minute with sustained demand; this is a **provider maximum**, not an entitlement of 10,000 usable DividendX sessions. Pro allows 8 vCPUs and 24-hour sessions, but this application intentionally provisions two vCPUs for 15 minutes. Vercel bills active CPU time, provisioned memory for wall time (one-minute minimum increments), creations, port traffic, and snapshot storage. The `iad1` rate is $0.128 per active vCPU-hour, $0.0212 per GB-hour provisioned, $0.60 per million creations, and $0.15 per GB Sandbox transfer. The source does not explicitly choose a Sandbox region, while `vercel.json` pins the Functions to `iad1`; verify the project Sandbox default/actual region before treating the `iad1` quote as an invoice forecast.

For a two-vCPU/four-GB VM held 15 minutes in `iad1`, memory costs **$0.0212 even while idle**. CPU adds between $0 and **$0.064** (the latter assumes both vCPUs busy the entire time), so the Sandbox compute-only interval is approximately **$0.0212–$0.0852 per full-length session**, plus port traffic, $0.0000006/creation, snapshot storage and Functions/Blob/CDN usage. In shorter or failed sessions, memory wall time and actual active CPU differ. The table assumes *every* session stays up 15 minutes, `iad1` prices, and no monthly credit or other charges:

| Sessions/day | Sandbox compute/day | Sandbox compute for 30 such days |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 | $2.12–$8.52 | $63.60–$255.60 |
| 500 | $10.60–$42.60 | $318–$1,278 |
| 1,000 | $21.20–$85.20 | $636–$2,556 |

The [Blob price and limits](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob/usage-and-pricing) include advanced operations (such as each conditional `put`) at $5/million in `iad1`, and Pro rate limit 4,500 advanced operations/minute (75/s). This ledger's CAS collisions and whole-object transfer add reads/retries, so this is not a reliable per-visitor cost or throughput model. Browser/API egress, server Function compute, snapshots, and other team projects are outside the table. Pro's $20 monthly usage credit is **shared across metered services** and on-demand billing continues after it ([Pro plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/pro-plan)). [Spend Management](https://vercel.com/docs/spend-management) alerts alone do not stop use; optional production pause applies to **all** team projects, may be checked every few minutes, and is not a strict instant per-project budget.

The read-only `vercel usage --scope payai` and `vercel usage --group-by project --scope payai` reports for 1–21 September showed a Pro team, DividendX project effective usage **$0.7890** and billed **$0.0000**: Sandbox provisioned memory $0.5138, Active CPU $0.0367, data transfer $0.0012, snapshot storage $0.0069; most of the remainder was $0.2135 build CPU. Team-wide effective usage was $56.8921 and billed $22.9218 (mostly plan/seats/build); project Blob operations appeared unattributed in that report. These are historical, team-shared billing observations, not a session cost benchmark. Actual account-specific Sandbox region, allocation headroom, current active reservations, Spend Management alert threshold and production-pause toggle were **not confirmed** by these CLI read-only views.

## Launch recommendation

Do **not** advertise unlimited concurrent tours or promise every social visitor immediate access. The current cap guarantees a fifth simultaneous wallet/guided request is refused; four full 15-minute sessions yield a theoretical 16 admissions/hour, and the 100 UTC daily starts can be spent much faster than a full day. A capacity of 8/16/32 full sessions would permit at most 32/64/128 admissions/hour in steady turnover, assuming perfectly spaced arrivals, successful boots and healthy storage. Spikes still produce honest 503s. Reset consumes another start; launch visitors arriving behind a demo already in progress cannot share that visitor's chain.

**Near-term bounded pilot:** preserve 15-minute VM, two-vCPU resource, deny-all network, cookie/IP quotas, mutation caps and uncertainty/reconciliation behavior. Reduce unnecessary guided state polling during ongoing transactions, retaining timely progress and the existing per-session request budget. Test a proposed 8-active, 250-starts/UTC-day profile with representative simultaneous guided+wallet visitors in a separately configured staging ledger/provider, including starts, polling, resets, expiries and provider timeouts. Choose whether to ship that profile only if a real-Blob bounded test shows negligible ledger 503/busy errors, acceptable readiness p95, accurate accounting and a reviewed daily Sandbox cost envelope (250 fully occupied `iad1` sessions: $5.30–$21.30 compute/day). Any larger 16/500 profile needs the meter separation below, controlled validation, and an explicit daily spend decision. Keep per-visitor six and IP 30 initially; IP 30 can constrain shared NAT/corporate audiences even when global capacity is available, so adjust only with measured legitimate collision and rate-abuse evidence. Preserve honest full/capped UI and a public-devnet/static explanation fallback; do not automatically hammer start retries.

**Smallest safe structural relief:** move per-session `forwardedTotal`, `minuteWindow/minuteCount`, and `mutationTotal` into a private **per-session CAS record** keyed by opaque session ID. Keep the global ledger for daily admission counters and active reservation/latest identity, so each normal `/state` read rewrites only that visitor's meter. Lifecycle state must still fence charges and reset: mark the per-session meter closing before stopping its VM, serialize admission against that flag, and ensure accepted in-flight requests are drained/fenced before stop; after a charge, verify the global latest/ready/expiry identity and provider identity before forwarding. Create/close/reconcile both records idempotently and fail closed after a partial write or uncertain provider result. Never make counters best-effort, silently release unknown capacity, or race an old ID into a new chain. This is roughly **2–4 engineering days plus targeted concurrency tests and a separately bounded staging exercise**, not a one-line quota edit. A later migration to an atomic per-key counter store could be evaluated if real-Blob throughput remains limiting.

Before social promotion, review the team's actual Sandbox region, usage dashboard, current active VMs, and Spend Management alerts/pause settings in the Vercel dashboard. Decide a daily sessions/spend envelope and whether interrupting every `payai` production project at a team threshold is acceptable. Obtain a bounded staging measurement of eight and then sixteen simultaneous visitors, capturing charge CAS attempt counts, 503/busy/429, Blob operation rate, startup p50/p95, per-flow request counts and actual Active CPU/Memory; stop at the first saturation signal. No production stress test or VM starts were performed here.
